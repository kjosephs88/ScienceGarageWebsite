Reading Material: Reactions Involving Acids and Bases

Chemical reactions involving acids and bases are common in industrial and consumer applications, natural processes, and in classroom experiments. Acids and bases undergo many reactions because they are able to react with each other as well as with other compounds and elements.

Reactions of Acids with Metals

Memory Jogger

Single-replacement reactions have the general formula: $\ce{A + BC -> AC + B}$.

Any element in the Activity Series of the Reference Tables for Physical Setting/Chemistry will react with the ion of any element below it. Hydrogen ($\ce{H2}$) is found near the bottom of the table. Thus, any metal above hydrogen in the table will react with the hydrogen ions in acids to produce $\ce{H2}$ and a salt.

$\ce{Zn(s) + 2HCl(aq) -> H2(g) + ZnCl2(aq)}$

Copper, which is below hydrogen in the table, will not react with hydrogen ions in acids.

Neutralization Reactions

Memory Jogger

When writing equations, first write correct formulas using charges and subscripts. Then, balance the equation using coefficients.

In a neutralization reaction, an Arrhenius acid reacts with an Arrhenius base to produce water and a salt. For example, consider the reaction between hydrochloric acid ($\ce{HCl}$) and sodium hydroxide ($\ce{NaOH}$).

Word Equation:

Hydrochloric acid + Sodium hydroxide $\rightarrow$ Water + Sodium chloride

Formula Equation:

$\ce{HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) -> H2O(\ell) + NaCl(aq)}$

Ionic Equation:

$\ce{H+(aq) + Cl^-(aq) + Na+(aq) + OH^-(aq) -> H2O(\ell) + Na+(aq) + Cl^-(aq)}$

Spectator ions (sodium and chloride) are present on both sides and can be omitted to yield the net ionic equation:

$\ce{H+(aq) + OH^-(aq) -> H2O(\ell)}$

Because hydrogen ions exist in solution as hydronium ions ($\ce{H3O+}$), this equation can also be written as:

$\ce{H3O+(aq) + OH^-(aq) -> 2H2O(\ell)}$

Sample Problem: Writing Neutralization Reactions

Sample Problem
Write the equation for the neutralization reaction between dilute nitric acid and potassium hydroxide.

Known

reactant 1 = nitric acid ($\ce{HNO3}$)

reactant 2 = potassium hydroxide ($\ce{KOH}$)


Unknown

neutralization equation = ?

1. Write a simple word equation:

acid + base $\rightarrow$ water + salt

2. Substitute known compounds:

$\ce{HNO3 + KOH -> water + salt}$

3. The $\ce{OH-}$ of the base and the $\ce{H+}$ of the acid combine to form water ($\ce{H2O}$):

$\ce{HNO3 + KOH -> H2O + salt}$

The remaining $\ce{K+}$ and $\ce{NO3-}$ ions combine to form the salt $\ce{KNO3}$.

$\ce{HNO3 + KOH -> H2O + KNO3}$

4. Now check to see that the equation is balanced. Because the hydroxide and hydrogen ions combine in a 1:1 ratio to form water molecules, they must be present in equal numbers. In this example, there is one hydroxide ion and one hydrogen ion, so the coefficients are both 1.

Complex Neutralizations

When a diprotic acid reacts with a dihydroxy base, two hydrogen ions and two hydroxide ions combine to form two molecules of water:

$\ce{Ca(OH)2 + H2SO4 -> 2H2O + CaSO4}$

If the ions are not equal, use coefficients to balance the equation:

Unbalanced:

$\ce{Mg(OH)2 + HCl -> H2O + MgCl2}$

Balanced:

$\ce{Mg(OH)2 + 2HCl -> 2H2O + MgCl2}$

Salts

Salts are ionic substances composed of positively charged metallic or polyatomic ions, and negative ions other than hydroxide ions. Sodium chloride ($\ce{NaCl}$) and ammonium phosphate ($\ce{(NH4)3PO4}$) are examples. Salts are named using the positive ion of the base and the negative ion of the acid.
