Background

A blood-spatter pattern is created when a wound is inflicted, and blood leaves the body. This pattern can help reconstruct the series of crime-scene events surrounding a shooting, stabbing, or beating.

Recall that blood forms droplets as it falls from a wound. A drop of blood that falls on a flat surface will not totally flatten out—the blood drop will have a domed surface. The reason for this shape is the internal cohesive nature of blood. Blood tends to pull together because of cohesion and resists flattening out on a surface.

The result is that the surface of the blood is elastic, giving the top of the blood spatter a rounded appearance. If any of the blood does overcome cohesion and separates from the main droplet of blood, it will form small secondary droplets known as satellites.

Cohesive forces give a domed shape to a blood droplet.

Materials & Procedure

Materials

  • Dropper bottle of simulated blood
  • Six 5x8 inch index cards
  • Three meter sticks
  • Ruler
  • Butcher paper or newspaper
  1. Chose a name for your investigation team (your lab partners).
  2. Label the six index cards with your team name at the top and the "drop height" of the blood drop, based on the values in the data table in the data section. See the example of a card in the image to the right.
  3. Place butcher paper on the floor in an appropriate area of 1 m².
  4. Place the index card marked with the lowest height value in the middle of the butcher paper.
  5. One investigation member should hold a meter stick vertically with the zero side on the floor right next to the index card.
  6. Another investigation member should hold the dropper bottle with the simulated blood at the first height in the data table.
  7. Carefully squeeze the dropper bottle so that one drop of simulated blood falls onto the surface of the index card. (You may want to practice dropping blood onto a blank sheet of paper first).
  8. Move the index card slightly to the side and repeat step 7, making sure that the second drop does not fall on the first.
  9. Repeat with a third drop.
  10. Carefully remove the index card from the butcher paper and place it in a location where it can dry undisturbed.
  11. Repeat dropping simulated blood from the other heights mentioned in the data table.
  12. After all simulated blood has dried on the cards, measure the width of each fallen drop to the nearest millimeter. Do not include any satellites or spines in your measurement.
