Chemistry of Underwater Welding - Part 1

Underwater welders often use "Thermite" reactions to repair steel ship hulls or pipelines. Because this reaction provides its own oxygen source within the reactants, it can proceed underwater without being extinguished. The reaction involves a mixture of powdered aluminum (\(\ce{Al}\)) and iron (III) oxide (\(\ce{Fe2O3}\)). This reaction is so energetic that the iron is produced in a liquid state at temperatures exceeding 2500°C, allowing it to flow into cracks and fuse metal parts together.

Unbalanced equation for this reaction:
\(\ce{Al(s) + Fe2O3(s) -> Al2O3(s) + Fe(l)}\)

1. When the equation is correctly balanced using the smallest whole-number coefficients, what is the total sum of the coefficients? (1 point)
  • a) 4
  • b) 5
  • c) 6
  • d) 7
Correct Answer: c) 6
2. Which type of reaction is represented by the welders thermite equation? (1 point)
  • a) Double Replacement
  • b) Combustion
  • c) Single Replacement
  • d) Synthesis
Correct Answer: c) Single Replacement
3. Based on the "Thermite reaction" discussed above, which element is undergoing oxidation? Choose the answer with the best explanation.
  • a) \(\ce{Al}\), because it gains electrons and its oxidation number goes from +3 to 0
  • b) \(\ce{Fe^{+3}}\), because is gains electrons and its oxidation number goes from +3 to 0
  • c) \(\ce{Al}\), because it loses electrons and its oxidation number goes from 0 to +3
  • d) \(\ce{Fe^{+3}}\), because is loses electrons and its oxidation number goes from 0 to +3
Correct Answer: c) \(\ce{Al}\), because it loses electrons and its oxidation number goes from 0 to +3.
Chemistry of Underwater Welding - Part 2

During underwater welding the aluminum powder can often become damp, and a side reaction occurs where the aluminum begins to react with water. When aluminum reacts with water it will produce aluminum oxide and hydrogen gas. This is shown by the balanced chemical equation below.

\(\ce{2Al(s) + 6H2O(l) -> 2Al(OH)3 + 3H2(g)}\)

Propane Grills

Propane grills are a staple of American outdoor cooking, valued for their convenience and clean-burning blue flame. Propane (\(\ce{C3H8}\)) is a byproduct of natural gas processing and petroleum refining. While it burns more cleanly than coal or wood, the combustion of propane is a significant source of anthropocentric carbon dioxide, a greenhouse gas that traps heat in the Earth’s atmosphere.

When a grill is functioning correctly with sufficient oxygen, it undergoes complete combustion, producing water vapor and \(\ce{CO2}\). However, if air vents are restricted, incomplete combustion occurs, producing carbon monoxide - a toxic, colorless gas - and "soot" (unburned carbon). As environmental concerns grow, many consumers are looking at alternatives like Electric Grills powered by renewable energy or Hydrogen-Fueled Grills, which produce only water vapor as a byproduct, effectively eliminating the carbon footprint of the cooking process.

Propane Grill Reaction: \(\ce{C3H8(g) + 5O2(g) -> 3CO2(g) + 4H2O(g) + heat}\)
Hydrogen-Fueled Grill Reaction: \(\ce{2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O + heat}\)

  • a) 12
  • b) 13
  • c) 14
  • d) 4
Correct Answer: b) 13

Identify if this mathematical representation is correct and explain how it demonstrates that mass is conserved. (1 point)
Answer: This is correct. The total mass of the reactants is 36.0 g and the total mass of the products is 36.0 g. Since the mass of reactants equals the mass of products, mass is conserved.

Airbag Chemistry - Part 1

When a car undergoes a sudden collision, a series of rapid chemical reactions occur to inflate an airbag in less than 0.05 seconds. The primary reaction involves the solid compound sodium azide (\(\ce{NaN3}\)). An electronic signal ignites the \(\ce{NaN3}\), causing it to decompose into solid sodium (\(\ce{Na}\)) and nitrogen gas (\(\ce{N2}\)).

The following unbalanced equation represents the chemical process inside the airbag:
Reaction 1: \(\ce{NaN3(s) -> Na(s) + N2(g)}\)

10. Based on the chemical equation provided, which type of chemical reaction occurs when sodium azide is ignited? (1 point)
  • a) Synthesis
  • b) Decomposition
  • c) Single replacement
  • d) Double replacement
Correct Answer: b) Decomposition
11. A technician determines that a deployed airbag contains \(3.01 \times 10^{23}\) molecules of nitrogen gas. Calculate the total number of moles of \(\ce{N2}\) present in the airbag. (1 point)
Result: 0.5 moles
12. Balance the Reaction 1: \(\ce{NaN3(s) -> Na(s) + N2(g)}\) (1 point)
Correct Answer: \(\ce{2NaN3(s) -> 2Na(s) + 3N2(g)}\)
13. Reaction 1 is a Redox Reaction. Identify who is reduced and write the half reaction. Be sure to include electrons. (2 point)
Answer: \(\ce{Na^{+}}\) is reduced. \(\ce{Na^{+} + 1e^{-} -> Na}\)
Airbag Chemistry - Part 2

The sodium metal (\(\ce{Na}\)) produced in the first reaction is highly reactive and potentially dangerous. To neutralize it, airbag manufacturers include potassium nitrate (\(\ce{KNO3}\)), which reacts with the sodium to produce more nitrogen gas and various metal oxides.

Reaction 2: \(\ce{10Na + 2KNO3 -> K2O + 5Na2O + N2}\)

14. In Reaction 2, the sodium atoms lose electrons to become \(\ce{Na^{+}}\) ions. Which statement best describes this process? (1 point)
  • a) It is a nuclear process...
  • b) It is a chemical process involving the transfer of electrons.
  • c) It is a physical change...
  • d) It is a phase change...
Correct Answer: b) It is a chemical process involving the transfer of electrons.
15. Based on patterns of the Periodic Table, why does sodium (\(\ce{Na}\)) react so readily to form \(\ce{Na^{+}}\) ions during these processes? (1 point)
  • a) It has a full outer energy level...
  • b) It needs to gain 7 electrons...
  • c) It has 1 valence electron that is easily removed...
  • d) Its nucleus contains more neutrons...
Correct Answer: c) It has 1 valence electron that is easily removed to achieve a stable configuration.
16. Predict the outcome if a different group 1 metal, such as lithium (\(\ce{Li}\)), was used instead of sodium (\(\ce{Na}\)) in a similar reaction to reaction 2. Explain your prediction using trends in the periodic table. (2 points)
Answer: Lithium will react in the same manner as sodium because they are in the same group.
