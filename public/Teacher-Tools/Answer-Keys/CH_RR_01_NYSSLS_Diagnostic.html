Stimulus 4 — Electron Energy Transitions

In atoms, electrons occupy specific energy levels. When electrons absorb energy they move to higher energy levels. When electrons return to lower energy levels, the energy difference is released as light (a photon).

6. Use the energy-level model in Stimulus 4 to explain how atoms produce colored light in fireworks.
Answer: The firework provides energy that electrons absorb, causing them to move to higher energy levels. When these electrons return to lower energy levels, they emit light energy (photons) of specific wavelengths, which we see as color.

Phenomenon 3: FIRE EXTINGUISHER \(\ce{CO2}\) PRODUCTION

Fire extinguishers are designed to stop fires by removing one of the conditions necessary for combustion. Some fire extinguishers produce carbon dioxide gas (\(\ce{CO2}\)) through a chemical reaction. The \(\ce{CO2}\) spreads around the flame and reduces the amount of oxygen available, helping to stop the fire.

One reaction used in some fire extinguishers involves sodium bicarbonate (\(\ce{NaHCO3}\)).

Stimulus 1 — How a \(\ce{CO2}\) Fire Extinguisher Works

The effectiveness of a fire extinguisher depends on how quickly reactions can be stopped. The diagram shows how sodium bicarbonate decomposes to produce carbon dioxide gas used in fire extinguishers.

\(\ce{2 NaHCO3 -> Na2CO3 + CO2 + H2O}\)

20. Which type of chemical reaction is represented when sodium bicarbonate breaks down into multiple products?
  • A. synthesis
  • B. decomposition
  • C. single replacement
  • D. double replacement
Correct Answer: B. decomposition
21. Which balanced equation correctly represents the decomposition of sodium bicarbonate?
  • A. \(\ce{NaHCO3 -> Na2CO3 + CO2 + H2O}\)
  • B. \(\ce{2 NaHCO3 -> Na2CO3 + CO2 + H2O}\)
  • C. \(\ce{NaHCO3 -> Na2CO3 + 2 CO2 + H2O}\)
  • D. \(\ce{2 NaHCO3 -> Na2CO3 + 2 CO2 + H2O}\)
Correct Answer: B. \(\ce{2 NaHCO3 -> Na2CO3 + CO2 + H2O}\)
22. Explain why chemical equations must be balanced. Refer to the conservation of atoms or conservation of mass.
Answer: According to the Law of Conservation of Mass, matter cannot be created or destroyed. Therefore, the number of each type of atom in the reactants must exactly equal the number of each type of atom in the products.
23. Determine the number of moles of \(\ce{CO2}\) produced when 2.0 moles of \(\ce{NaHCO3}\) react.
Mathematical Representation: \(2.0\text{ moles } \ce{NaHCO3} \times \left( \frac{1\text{ mole } \ce{CO2}}{2\text{ moles } \ce{NaHCO3}} \right)\)
Result: 1.0 mole of \(\ce{CO2}\)
24. Which change would most effectively slow down the combustion reaction?
  • A. increasing temperature
  • B. increasing oxygen concentration
  • C. decreasing particle collisions
  • D. increasing surface area
Correct Answer: C. decreasing particle collisions
25. Explain how reducing collisions between particles affects the rate of a chemical reaction.
Answer: According to Collision Theory, a reaction only occurs when particles collide with sufficient energy and proper orientation. Reducing the frequency of collisions directly decreases the number of successful reactions per second, thereby slowing the reaction rate.

Phenomenon 5: Dissolving Salts: Why Some Solutions Get Cold

When some salts dissolve in water, the temperature of the solution decreases. For example, ammonium nitrate (\(\ce{NH4NO3}\)) is used in instant cold packs because the process of dissolving absorbs energy from the surroundings. In contrast, sodium chloride (\(\ce{NaCl}\)) dissolves with only a small temperature change.

Stimulus 1 — Temperature vs Time for Dissolving \(\ce{NH4NO3}\)
39. Which statement best describes the energy change during the dissolving process?
  • A. Energy is released to the surroundings
  • B. Energy is absorbed from the surroundings
  • C. No energy transfer occurs
  • D. Energy is converted to light
Correct Answer: B. Energy is absorbed from the surroundings.
40. Based on the graph, which conclusion is best supported?
  • A. The solution is heating up
  • B. The dissolving process absorbs energy
  • C. The dissolving process forms a gas
  • D. The dissolving process releases heat
Correct Answer: B. The dissolving process absorbs energy (as evidenced by the drop in temperature of the surrounding water).
41. Explain why the temperature decreases as \(\ce{NH4NO3}\) dissolves in water. In your answer, describe how energy moves between the system and the surroundings.
Answer: Dissolving ammonium nitrate is an endothermic process. The chemical "system" absorbs thermal energy from the "surroundings" (the water). Because the water loses energy, its temperature decreases.
Stimulus 2 — Particle Model of Dissolution

Water molecules surround and stabilize ions during dissolution.

42. Which statement best describes what happens during dissolution?
  • A. Water molecules break into atoms
  • B. Ions are surrounded by water molecules
  • C. New substances are formed
  • D. Ions disappear
Correct Answer: B. Ions are surrounded by water molecules (Hydration).
43. Use the particle model to explain why dissolving \(\ce{NH4NO3}\) requires energy. In your answer, describe interactions that are broken and formed.
Answer: Energy is required to break the strong ionic bonds within the \(\ce{NH4NO3}\) crystal lattice and the hydrogen bonds between water molecules. While energy is released when new ion-dipole attractions form, the energy absorbed to break the old bonds is greater than the energy released, resulting in a net absorption of energy.
Stimulus 3 — Potential Energy Diagram

  • A. Energy is released and products have lower energy
  • B. Energy is absorbed and products have higher energy
  • C. Energy is released and products have higher energy
  • D. No energy change occurs
Correct Answer: B. Energy is absorbed and products have higher energy.
45. Explain how the energy diagram and temperature graph together provide evidence that dissolving \(\ce{NH4NO3}\) is endothermic.
Answer: The temperature graph shows a decrease in the temperature of the surroundings, meaning energy was lost to the system. The potential energy diagram shows the products have higher potential energy than the reactants, confirming the system gained that energy.
