5. Propose an explanation for why the mass of dissolved solute changed among the three Experiments.
The mass of the dissolved solute increased across the experiments because the mass of the solvent (water) increased, providing more space/particles for the solute to dissolve into.
6. Would it be acceptable for a student to use Trial 2 from Experiment 1 to determine the solubility?
No. In Trial 2, all 2.0g of the added solute dissolved, meaning the solution is unsaturated. To find the true solubility limit, the solution must be saturated (maxed out).

10. Devise a well-constructed response to this claim. (Claim: Exp 3 Trial 9 18.0g dissolves, Exp 1 Trial 9 3.6g dissolves. Obviously, solubility is greater in Exp 3.)
The student is confusing the total mass dissolved with the actual rate of solubility. Solubility is a ratio of solute to solvent. Because Experiment 3 uses 5 times as much water (50.0g) as Experiment 1 (10.0g), it dissolves 5 times as much solute (18.0g vs 3.6g). Both simplify to the same solubility ratio of 36g / 100g H₂O.

14a. Which beakers represent unsaturated solutions?
Beakers A and B, because if more solute were added, the solution would still be capable of dissolving it (the dissolved particle count hasn't capped at 18 yet).
14b. Which beakers represent saturated solutions?
Beakers C, D, and E, because they hold the maximum amount of dissolved particles (18).
15a. When a small amount of additional solute is added to an unsaturated solution...
The number of dissolved particles increases (as seen moving from Beaker A's 5 particles to Beaker B's 10 particles).
15b. When a small amount of additional solute is added to a saturated solution...
The number of dissolved particles stays the same while the number of solid particles increases (as seen moving from Beaker C to D to E, dissolved stays at 18 while solid increases).
15c. Predict what would happen if a small amount of additional solute were stirred into beaker E.
The number of dissolved particles would remain exactly at 18, and all newly added solute would simply sink to the bottom, increasing the solid particle count.
16. Summarize the meaning of the word saturated.
Saturated means holding as much water or moisture as can be absorbed; thoroughly soaked. In chemistry, it refers to a solution holding the maximum amount of dissolved solute possible at a given temperature.
17. What feature would typically enable a student to distinguish a saturated solution?
The presence of solid, undissolved solute remaining at the bottom of the beaker.
18. What simple test could you perform to determine the answer? (If handed a clear beaker with no solid at the bottom)
Add a tiny crystal or pinch of the solute. If it dissolves, the solution was unsaturated. If it falls to the bottom and remains a solid, the solution was already saturated.
