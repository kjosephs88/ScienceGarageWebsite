Lab 17: Mole Stations
MASTER ANSWER KEY

Station 1: Aluminum Moles and Atoms (3 pts)
Assumed measurement: 0.45 g Al.
1. Is this sample one mole of aluminum? (1 pt)
Answer: NO. One mole of aluminum is 26.98 g.
2. Does this sample have approximately \(6.02 \times 10^{23}\) atoms? (2 pts)
Answer: NO. \(0.45\text{ g Al} \times \frac{1\text{ mol Al}}{26.98\text{ g Al}} \times \frac{6.022 \times 10^{23}\text{ atoms Al}}{1\text{ mol Al}} = 1.0 \times 10^{22}\text{ atoms}\)
Station 2: Baking Soda/Vinegar Molar Volume (5 pts)
Assumed mass loss of \(\ce{CO2}\): 0.35 g.
1. Mass of gas released. (1 pt)
Result: 0.35 g
2. Moles of \(\ce{CO2}\) gas released. (2 pts)
Result: 0.00795 moles \(0.35\text{ g CO}_2 \times \frac{1\text{ mol CO}_2}{44.01\text{ g CO}_2} = 0.00795\text{ mol}\)
3. Volume of gas at STP. (2 pts)
Result: 0.178 Liters \(0.00795\text{ mol CO}_2 \times \frac{22.4\text{ L CO}_2}{1\text{ mol CO}_2} = 0.178\text{ L}\)
Station 3: Copper(II) Sulfate Pentahydrate (5 pts)
Assumed measurement: 5.25 g.
1. Molar mass of \(\ce{CuSO4 \cdot 5H2O}\). (1 pt)
Result: 249.68 g/mol
2. Moles of \(\ce{CuSO4 \cdot 5H2O}\). (2 pts)
Result: 0.0210 moles \(5.25\text{ g} \times \frac{1\text{ mol}}{249.68\text{ g}} = 0.0210\text{ mol}\)
3. Number of formula units. (2 pts)
Result: \(1.26 \times 10^{22}\) formula units \(0.0210\text{ mol} \times \frac{6.022 \times 10^{23}\text{ units}}{1\text{ mol}} = 1.26 \times 10^{22}\text{ units}\)
Station 4: Sugar in a Snickers Bar (4 pts)
Assumed mass from label: 27 g.
1. Moles of sucrose (\(\ce{C12H22O11}\)). (2 pts)
Result: 0.0789 moles \(27\text{ g C}_{12}\ce{H}_{22}\ce{O}_{11} \times \frac{1\text{ mol}}{342.30\text{ g}} = 0.0789\text{ mol}\)
2. Molecules of sucrose. (2 pts)
Result: \(4.75 \times 10^{22}\) molecules \(0.0789\text{ mol} \times \frac{6.022 \times 10^{23}\text{ molecules}}{1\text{ mol}} = 4.75 \times 10^{22}\text{ molecules}\)
Station 5: Atoms in Chalk (6 pts)
Assumed mass used: 0.12 g.
1. Moles of chalk (\(\ce{CaCO3}\)). (1 pt)
Result: 0.0012 moles \(0.12\text{ g CaCO}_3 \times \frac{1\text{ mol}}{100.09\text{ g}} = 0.0012\text{ mol}\)
2. Number of formula units. (2 pts)
Result: \(7.23 \times 10^{20}\) units \(0.0012\text{ mol} \times \frac{6.022 \times 10^{23}\text{ units}}{1\text{ mol}} = 7.23 \times 10^{20}\text{ units}\)
3. Number of Oxygen atoms. (3 pts)
Result: \(2.17 \times 10^{21}\) atoms \(7.23 \times 10^{20}\text{ formula units} \times \frac{3\text{ Oxygen atoms}}{1\text{ formula unit}} = 2.17 \times 10^{21}\text{ atoms}\)
Station 6: Sugar in Gum (5 pts)
Assumed sugar mass (Unchewed - Chewed): 1.50 g.

2. Molecules of sugar. (3 pts)
Result: \(2.64 \times 10^{21}\) molecules \(0.00438\text{ mol} \times \frac{6.022 \times 10^{23}\text{ molecules}}{1\text{ mol}} = 2.64 \times 10^{21}\text{ molecules}\)

Station 8: Oreo Percent Composition (6 pts)
Assumed Regular (11.3g total, 3.1g creme) | Double (14.5g total, 6.2g creme).
1. % Creme in Regular Oreo. (2 pts)
Result: 27.4% \(\frac{3.1\text{ g creme}}{11.3\text{ g total}} \times 100 = 27.4\%\)
2. % Creme in Double Stuf Oreo. (2 pts)
Result: 42.8% \(\frac{6.2\text{ g creme}}{14.5\text{ g total}} \times 100 = 42.8\%\)
3. CER Conclusion: Does it live up to the name? (2 pts)
Answer: Students must use their data to argue if the mass or percentage reflects "Double." Example: While the creme mass doubled (3.1g to 6.2g), the total percentage of the cookie only increased from 27% to 43%.
